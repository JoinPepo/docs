This is a **`test` key on a sample workspace** — the same key type you would use against your own brand, pointed at a demo one. It never meters, never collects, and is read-only, so it is safe to publish: it cannot run up a bill or change the workspace it showcases.

Two labels travel with every response, and they mean different things. `test_mode: true` says nothing was charged. `demo: true` says the videos and creators are a sample rather than a real brand's — that one comes from the workspace, so a test key on *your* workspace would not carry it.

## The demo script

Seven prompts. Ask them in order — each shows something the last one did not, and **most of them include the product declining to answer part of what was asked**, which is the part worth watching.

<Steps>
  <Step title="It uses the corpus, not the web">
    > What is trending in our category right now?

    Note what did *not* happen: no web search, no general trend piece. The answer is about the videos this workspace collected, and every ranked video carries the observation count behind its score. Nothing is called *trending* on too few snapshots, however large its view count.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Views are not impressions">
    > How does our share of voice compare with competitors this week?

    Watch for the refusal to use the word *impressions*. Views are observed content views; an impression is an ad-delivery event, and a media agency prices budget against the difference. Posts naming both you and a competitor are counted for neither side.
  </Step>
  <Step title="A number it will not stand behind">
    > Which competitor gained the most ground in the last 7 days?

    On this corpus the answer is *"do not read this as market movement"* — the two periods were not observed to comparable depth, so the change measures the collection rather than the market. It then tells you **which window would work**, so the next question is a re-run rather than a dead end.

    > Try it again with the window it suggested.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Two mentions are not a finding">
    > Any complaints about the Glow Serum I should escalate to product?

    This shows the evidence floor working in **both** directions. Some themes clear it and come back as findings, each with its source count, its distinct-**voice** count and verbatim quotes. Others do not, and those come back in a separate list that is deliberately **not chartable**.

    The distinction to point at: several comments from one account is one person, so a theme needs three distinct sources **and** two distinct voices before Pepo will call it a finding. Ranking an unestablished theme beside an established one on the same bar chart is exactly the mistake the split exists to prevent.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Rank order will mislead you">
    > Which creators should we send product to?

    The highest median views in the roster belongs to a creator whose evidence level is only `provisional` — too few posts for the number to mean much, with one breakout doing the work. Watch it surface the steadier `measured` creators underneath, and watch what it says about safety: no creator matched an exclusion term, and that is **not** a clearance.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Paid versus organic, refused">
    > How did our launch perform, and how much of the lift was paid?

    It splits owned versus not-owned and refuses to split paid from earned, because nothing in a social corpus records whether a post was promoted, boosted, gifted or contracted. It also refuses to call any of it *lift* — there is no control group.
  </Step>
  <Step title="What is wrong with the data">
    > What videos do we actually have, and what is wrong with them?

    Gaps, duplicates, coverage, and a verification block that distinguishes *complete* from *confirmed*: fields are present and ids are unique, but nothing has been re-checked against the platform since collection — so an empty gap list is not a clean bill of health.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What you are looking at

The sample workspace is a skincare brand with competitors, products and a connected owned channel. It is **not a real customer** — the brand is named "Lumi Beauty (Pepo demo — sample data)" for that reason, and every response repeats it.

Two limits worth knowing before you judge the output:

- **The corpus is small**, a few hundred videos over a few days. Several flows refuse to draw conclusions at that size, which is correct behaviour and also means you see the refusals more often than a real workspace would.
- **Test keys cannot collect.** Collection calls a paid provider, so the sample corpus is fixed rather than growing on your request. Everything else works exactly as it does on a metered key.

The demo corpus is refreshed, so exactly which themes clear the evidence floor changes between runs. The *shape* of the answer — some established, some explicitly not, and the boundary shown rather than hidden — is the thing to look at.

## Then what

`POST /v1/estimate` prices any call before you make it. On a demo key the usage block reports `metered_usd` — what the call *would* have cost — while charging nothing, so you can see real pricing without being billed. That is the number to look at if you are working out whether this is affordable at your volume.

This key is rate limited to 30 requests a minute. Test keys have no balance to draw down, so a request limit is the only thing bounding a shared one.
