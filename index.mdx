Pepo keeps a library of public TikTok and Instagram video, plus public Reddit threads, for your workspace. Every video is analyzed once when it arrives: a timed transcript, a record of what is on screen, and a summary of what the creator says and how they feel about it. After that you can search it, count it and question it as often as you like.

The library covers the video people post about you and your competitors, including the far larger volume where a brand is discussed or shown without being tagged. Your own posts are recognized and set aside: they are never counted as what people think.

You work with it by talking to an agent in Claude or ChatGPT and following up, the way you would brainstorm with an analyst who has watched every video. Or you call the API and build it into your own product.

```
What do people complain about in the knee sleeve videos,
and do the ones that show the product being worn say something different?
```

## What makes the answers usable

**You can see what is behind every answer.** An answer to a question cites the video and the clip it came from. A momentum score arrives with how many observations it rests on, how many hours they span, and how many comparable videos it was ranked against. When someone asks where a claim came from, you can answer.

**Size and momentum are different questions.** A video with a lot of views is a big video. Whether something is *happening* is a claim about trajectory, and Pepo scores each video against comparable ones and against the creator's own normal, so a 40k-view video from a small creator can outrank an 800k-view video from a mega-account.

**One video is not a trend.** The cluster verdict is reported separately from the ranking, so you know whether you are looking at one creator having a big week or a format multiple independent creators are picking up.

**It tells you when it does not know.** A question no video could answer (who the audience is, what sold, what was spent on ads) comes back as a refusal with the reason, not a guess. Views are observed content views, never ad impressions, and nothing here measures paid media, sales or demographics.
