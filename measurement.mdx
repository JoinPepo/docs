A video is not trending because it has a lot of views. Views measure size; trending is a claim about a **trajectory**, and a trajectory needs more than one observation.

This page is the method, in full. It's public because you should be able to check it — a score you can't interrogate is a score you can't defend when someone asks.

This is the part most often misread: a video is held at `watchlist` for lack of observation **regardless of how high it scores**, and regardless of how many views it has.

## The comparison group

Every video is scored as a **percentile within a group of comparable videos**, never as a raw rate. The group is built from the same platform, similar topic or category, similar publishing age, similar creator size, same geography where available, and organic compared with organic.

This matters more than the weights. Raw view velocity is "many views, faster," which structurally favours large accounts — the videos you already knew about. Percentile ranking is what lets a 40k-view video from a 5,000-follower creator outrank an 800k-view video from a mega-account, which is usually the one worth acting on.

Every result reports `comparison_group.n`. A small `n` makes any percentile weak, so the number is returned rather than hidden.

## The score

| Component | Weight | What it measures |
|---|---|---|
| View-velocity percentile | 30% | Growth rate, ranked within the comparison group |
| Engagement-velocity percentile | 20% | Interaction growth, same ranking |
| Creator-baseline lift | 25% | Multiple of this creator's own normal performance |
| Acceleration | 15% | Whether growth is still speeding up |
| Share and comment quality | 10% | Conversation rather than applause |

**Creator-baseline lift is the least gameable signal** — it asks whether this video is unusual *for this creator*, which no amount of follower count fakes — so it carries more weight than raw velocity.

When a platform does not expose a metric, the remaining components are reweighted **and the result is marked lower confidence**. A reweighted score is not as good as a complete one and does not pretend to be.

## The gate

Bands alone are not enough, so classification is gated on observation before it is decided by score:

<Warning>
Fewer than **3 metric snapshots** or under **12 hours** of observation caps the classification at `watchlist` — regardless of score, and regardless of view count.
</Warning>

When that fires, `gated_by` explains it in words you can quote:

```json
"gated_by": "Capped at watchlist: 2 snapshot(s) over 3h — \"trending\" requires at least 3 across 12h."
```

This is the rule that most often surprises people, because the video it catches is frequently the biggest one on the list. That is the point. A video with 800k views and three hours of history might be a genuine breakout or might be a paid push that flattens by morning, and nothing in the data yet distinguishes them.

## The bands

| Classification | Requires |
|---|---|
| `insufficient_data` | Fewer than 2 observations — no trajectory exists to measure |
| `watchlist` | Score of 70+, or anything gated |
| `trending` | Score 80+, top 10% of the comparison group, ≥1.5× the creator's baseline, growth across consecutive snapshots |
| `breakout` | Score 90+, top 2%, ≥3× baseline, still accelerating |
| `peaked` | Previously growing; velocity has fallen more than 40% across two consecutive periods |
| `fading` | Below normal velocity and declining across multiple periods |

`peaked` and `fading` are reported even under the gate. Telling a brand a trend is over is a lower-stakes claim than telling them one exists, so it faces a lower evidence bar — deliberately asymmetric, in the direction that costs less when wrong.

## Viral is not a trend

Reported separately from the per-video ranking, in the `trend` object:

<CardGroup cols={3}>
  <Card title="viral_video">
    One video dramatically outperforming. Interesting, but not a movement.
  </Card>
  <Card title="emerging_trend">
    At least 5 relevant videos from 3 independent creators, accelerating.
  </Card>
  <Card title="confirmed_trend">
    At least 15 videos from 8 creators, cluster in the top 5% for velocity, and **no single creator generating more than 50% of its views**.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

That last clause is what stops one mega-creator's post being sold to you as a movement. When it fires, the verdict is downgraded and the reason is stated:

```json
"reasons": ["One creator accounts for 77% of cluster views — reported as emerging, not confirmed."]
```

## Confidence

`confidence` is a function of observation depth, metric completeness, and comparison-group size:

- **low** — under the gate, two or more metrics missing, or fewer than 20 comparable videos
- **medium** — gate cleared, one metric missing, or fewer than 50 comparable
- **high** — 5+ snapshots over 24h+, complete metrics, a substantial comparison group

Read it before the score. `87 / low confidence / 2 snapshots` and `87 / high confidence / 6 snapshots` are not the same finding, and only one of them belongs in a deck.

## Two things Pepo won't call something

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Views are not impressions">
    Public video views are observed content views — potential exposures. Real ad impressions need first-party or Ads Manager data. Responses carrying view-derived numbers say so, because that's the distinction most likely to get lost between an API and a slide.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Unobserved is not zero">
    A metric that was never observed comes back as `null` with its coverage attached. An empty corpus is reported as an empty corpus. `0` means measured and zero.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Why history has to accumulate

Platforms expose *current* counts, not past ones. There is no way to buy back the observations of a video from last Tuesday.

So a newly connected workspace genuinely cannot answer trending questions yet, and the API says so rather than scoring off a single observation:

<Warning>
**No metric history exists yet for this workspace**, so nothing can be scored for momentum — these are ranked by current engagement only. Trend scoring needs at least 3 snapshots across 12 hours per post.
</Warning>

That text arrives in the `caveat` field of the response, verbatim, so it survives being read by an assistant and repeated to you.

Coverage builds as [collection runs](/corpus): young posts are re-observed every few hours, so a post typically becomes watchlist-grade within a day and trend-grade within two or three. `corpus.posts_trend_grade` tells you how many posts have cleared that bar at any moment.
