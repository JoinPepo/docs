title: "Brand profile"
description: "The one setup step that decides whether results are about your brand or about your category."
---

Every workflow reads your brand profile before it does anything. It is what turns "find trending nail videos" into "find trending nail videos that matter to *this* brand, its products, and its competitors — and never surface the topics it has asked us to avoid."

Without one, the API refuses the call:

```json
{
  "error": {
    "type": "profile_required",
    "message": "This workspace has no approved brand profile. Every workflow reads it, so results would be generic category noise rather than answers about your brand.",
    "next": {
      "endpoint": "POST /v1/brand-profile/propose",
      "required_fields": ["brand_name", "website"]
    }
  }
}
```

That is deliberate. Returning plausible generic results would be worse than refusing — you would have no way to tell them apart from real ones.

## What it holds

| Field | Why it earns its place |
|---|---|
| `brand_names` | Spelling variants and nicknames. Consumers rarely write your brand the way your style guide does. |
| `products` | SKUs, lines, shades, flavours. The level a brand team actually acts on. |
| `competitors` | Turns "who is winning" into a question with a defined answer set. |
| `category` | Defines the comparison group a trend is measured against. |
| `customer_problems` | What people are trying to solve when they find you. Usually better search vocabulary than your own marketing language. |
| `audiences`, `markets` | Geography and segment filters. |
| `creators`, `campaigns`, `hashtags` | Known relationships, so earned coverage can be told apart from paid. |
| `exclusions` | Topics you refuse to be associated with. |

### Exclusions do more work than they look like

`exclusions` is the field most often left empty and the one whose absence is most visible later. It is enforced server-side in every workflow — matching results are withheld before they reach you, and counted in `corpus.excluded_by_brand_profile` so the filtering is never silent.

It is the difference between a trend brief a VP can forward and one that surfaces a competitor's product recall as a content opportunity.

Worth asking directly when you set this up: *is there anything we never want appearing in a brief — a lawsuit, a recall, a discontinued line, a competitor we would rather not amplify?*

## How it gets created

An agent researches your brand and website and **proposes** a profile; a person approves or corrects it. The approval is recorded against the version.

The review step is not ceremony. A profile an agent wrote and nobody checked makes every downstream answer wrong in the same direction, quietly, for months — and profile mistakes are the hardest kind to notice, because the results still look like results.

## Versioning

Every approved profile has a version, and **every result records which version produced it** (`brand_profile_version`).

Two runs against different versions are not strictly comparable. If a competitor was added on Tuesday, Monday's share-of-voice number and Wednesday's are answering slightly different questions — and the version stamp is what lets you notice that six weeks later, when the two numbers disagree in a meeting.

Editing creates a new version rather than mutating the old one. Previous versions are retained.

## Endpoints

```bash
GET /v1/brand-profile    # the approved profile, or needs_setup
```

<Note>
`POST /v1/brand-profile/propose` and `/approve` exist as internal functions and are not yet exposed over HTTP. During the pilot, profile setup happens in the dashboard or through the `pepo-setup-brand` MCP prompt. This page describes the shape you will get when they ship; the `GET` is live today.
</Note>
