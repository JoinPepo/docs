Your **library** is the set of public social video Pepo holds for your workspace: TikTok and Instagram video, public Reddit threads, their creators, captions and comments, each video's analysis, and a running history of how engagement moved. The API calls it the corpus. Everything Pepo answers is answered from it.

It is built by searching for what people post, not by watching what you post.

## What goes in, and what is set aside

  <Card title="Your own accounts, set aside" icon="user-slash">
    The handles you declare as yours, and your competitors' own handles, in the [brand profile](/brand-profile). Posts from them are recognized and withheld from every answer: a brand's own posts are never signal.
  </Card>

This is why the [brand profile](/brand-profile) earns its place. **Your products, competitors and customer problems become the searches.** A brand does not get tracked by watching `@brand`; it gets tracked by searching what people actually type: the product lines, the comparisons against rivals, the complaints.

A brand's set of subjects typically runs to a dozen or more searches, covering:

| Search shape | Finds |
|---|---|
| `<brand> <product line>` | People discussing specific products |
| `<brand> review` | Considered opinion, the highest-intent content |
| `<brand> vs <competitor>` | Head-to-head comparison, where switching happens |
| `<brand> <problem>` | Complaints and support moments before they reach you |

Before a search runs you can preview it: one page of what the platforms return, and how much of it actually names the subject. That matters for a person, a band or an unusual word, where keyword search matches something merely similar.

Every hit is then **checked against the subject before it counts.** A result that is not actually about it is excluded rather than padding the library, which is what keeps "posts considered" a number that means something. Near-duplicates are clustered, so one video reposted ten times is read once.

## Then each video is analyzed, once

Each kept video is analyzed when it arrives: a timed transcript, a record of what is on screen (the format, the setting, who appears, the products shown and how, text overlays, and a timed log of scenes), and a summary of what the creator says and how they feel about the main thing the video is about. It is then added to the index questions are answered from.

That work is done once and kept. A question asked later is answered from it, so asking a hundred questions does not pay to watch the videos a hundred times.

## Then comments

For posts that have them, Pepo collects the comment threads: **comment-richest posts first**. That ordering is deliberate: comments are where consumers say what they actually think, and prioritising the busiest threads means you get the widest view of the conversation for a given amount of collection.

Comment tone and coverage are what you see in [`GET /v1/videos/{video_id}`](/api-reference/library/get-one-video).

## Then, repeatedly, the metrics

This is the part that makes trend detection possible at all.

Collection doesn't stop at first capture. Pepo re-observes posts on a decaying cadence, frequently while a video is young and its trajectory is still forming, then less often as it settles:

| Post age | Re-observed |
|---|---|
| 0 to 3 days | Every 4 hours |
| 3 to 7 days | Every 8 hours |
| 7 to 14 days | Daily |
| Older | Weekly |

Within a cycle, busier posts go first: a post's place in the queue is weighted by its engagement, with comments and shares counting for more than views, since those are the signals that indicate something is actually spreading.

Each observation is appended, never overwritten. That series is the evidence behind every momentum score, and it's why [a video needs three observations across twelve hours](/measurement) before Pepo will call it trending.

<Note>
Platforms expose a video's **current** counts, not its past ones. There is no way to buy back last Tuesday's observations. This is the one part of Pepo that can't be sped up: a corpus that started collecting yesterday knows what happened yesterday, and a corpus that has been running for a month knows a month.
</Note>

## Reruns add, they don't duplicate

Collection is additive and idempotent. Posts are keyed on platform and post id; re-running discovery merges rather than duplicating, and posts whose comments are already held are skipped. Widening your discovery queries later deepens the corpus instead of forking it.

## What you get to control

| | |
|---|---|
| **Official accounts** | Your handles and your competitors', withheld from every answer |
| **Subjects** | What Pepo searches for. Derived from your brand profile, and tunable |
| **Platforms** | Which platforms to collect from |
| **Exclusions** | Topics withheld from every result. See [Brand profile](/brand-profile) |

You can add video for a new subject at any time on a budget you set, or schedule it to keep growing with a monthly cap. During the pilot the first set is configured with you at onboarding.

## How this shows up in answers

Every response tells you what it read:

```json
"corpus": {
  "posts_considered": 412,
  "posts_with_history": 388,
  "posts_trend_grade": 121,
  "excluded_by_brand_profile": 3
}
```

- **`posts_considered`**: how many posts this call passed over. It's also what the call is billed on; see [Credits](/credits).
- **`posts_with_history`**: how many have more than one observation.
- **`posts_trend_grade`**: how many have enough history to be classified beyond `watchlist`. This number rises on its own as observations accumulate, and it's the one to watch in a new workspace.
- **`excluded_by_brand_profile`**: withheld by your exclusions, counted so the filtering is never silent.
