Prompts are the part of MCP that most servers skip. They are user-controlled templates your host surfaces as slash commands, a command palette, or buttons — so the moment Pepo is connected, your team can see what it does without opening this site.

In Claude, type `/` in a new chat.

## The five

<ResponseField name="/pepo-setup-brand" type="brand_name, website">
  Builds the brand profile every other workflow reads. Start here.

  It checks for an existing profile first, drafts products, competitors, category, audiences and exclusions, then **asks you to confirm before saving**. It asks about exclusions directly, because that is the field people leave empty and later wish they had not.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="/pepo-trending" type="window, platforms, limit">
  What is growing, with the evidence.

  Prices the call first, then reports results under explicit rules: only call something trending if its classification says so, always give the confidence and snapshot count with a score, and keep the cluster verdict separate from the ranking. Ends by asking which one is worth acting on this week.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="/pepo-search" type="query, platforms, window">
  Find specific videos by topic, product or creator.

  Carries the search-coverage caveat into the answer, so a nil result comes back as "nothing matched in captions" rather than "there are no such videos" — the difference matters if you are about to tell someone a topic has no coverage.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="/pepo-video-deep-dive" type="video_id">
  Everything about one video: how engagement moved across the observed snapshots, what the comments say, and what to take from it.

  Requires tone to be reported with its coverage. "Mostly positive" over three classified comments is a fabrication with a true-sounding shape.
</ResponseField>

<ResponseField name="/pepo-credits" type="">
  Balance, per-call ceiling, and roughly how many runs that supports at your current settings. Free.
</ResponseField>

## Why these are worded the way they are

Each prompt states the honesty constraints **before** the assistant sees any data, so it inherits them rather than being corrected afterwards. `/pepo-trending` includes, verbatim:

> Only call something trending if its `classification` says so. A result with `gated_by` set has too little observation history to support the claim — say "watchlist" and say why.

> "Momentum 87" on its own is not a finding.

Instructions given up front shape how a model reads results. The same warning added after the fact usually does not survive summarisation into a two-line answer, which is exactly the form these findings reach a VP in.

## Writing your own

Prompts are a convenience, not a gate — you can ask for anything in plain language. Two habits make the answers better:

**Ask for the evidence explicitly.** *"…and tell me how many snapshots each one is based on"* costs nothing and changes what gets repeated onward.

**Say what the answer is for.** *"I'm presenting this to our CMO on Thursday"* gets you the coverage caveats and the source links unprompted, because the assistant can tell the claim will be challenged.
